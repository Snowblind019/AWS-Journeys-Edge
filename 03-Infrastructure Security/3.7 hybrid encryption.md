# 3.7 — Hybrid Connectivity Encryption (MACsec, IPsec over DX, VPN)

**Domain 3: Infrastructure Security (18%)**
**Companion to:** `scs-c03-lab-3_7-hybrid-encryption.md` (the runnable lab). This file records what was worked through and *why each answer wins*, one level deeper than the lab's terse lines.

**Cost:** about $0. This subdomain is reasoning and config construction rather than a live build, since Direct Connect, MACsec, and Site-to-Site VPN all involve real provisioning and charges.

---

## What I worked through

- Started from the **wrong assumption** that Direct Connect alone covers encryption in transit, and saw why it does not: DX is private, but the Ethernet link is plaintext.
- Compared the two encryption fixes at **different layers**: MACsec at L2 for the link, and IPsec VPN over DX at L3 for end-to-end.
- Placed **Site-to-Site VPN over the internet** as the cheap encrypted failover, and **Transit Gateway** as the hub that ties VPCs, VPN, and Direct Connect together.

The point was that Direct Connect gives privacy, not encryption, and the exam's job is picking the right encryption layer for the constraint, link-level MACsec versus end-to-end IPsec, with throughput and connection type as the deciding factors.

---

## The model this proves

Direct Connect bypasses the public internet and is private, but private is not encrypted. The Ethernet link is plaintext, so passive wiretapping or a compromised intermediate segment would expose the data, and an audit finding stands until encryption is added. Two fixes operate at different layers:

- **MACsec** (IEEE 802.1AE) is Layer 2 link encryption, implemented in hardware on the router ASIC so it runs at near line rate. That speed is the reason it exists, since a single IPsec tunnel caps around 1.25 Gbps and high-bandwidth links would otherwise be throttled by encryption. It encrypts the entire Ethernet frame, including control-plane traffic like BGP and ARP. But it is point-to-point and hop-by-hop, so it only protects the segment between your router and the AWS Direct Connect router at the colocation, not end-to-end into the VPC.
- **IPsec VPN over Direct Connect** is Layer 3 and gives end-to-end encryption from the customer gateway to the AWS VPN endpoint. It works on both hosted and dedicated connections and combines IPsec confidentiality with the consistent latency of Direct Connect, at the cost of about 1.25 Gbps per tunnel.

| Option | Layer | Scope | Throughput | Works on | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MACsec | L2 link (802.1AE) | your router to the DX PoP only | near line rate (10/100/400G) | dedicated only, select PoPs | high-bandwidth link encryption |
| IPsec VPN over DX | L3 | end-to-end, CGW to AWS VPN endpoint | ~1.25 Gbps per tunnel | hosted and dedicated | end-to-end confidentiality over DX |
| Site-to-Site VPN over internet | L3 | end-to-end | ~1.25 Gbps per tunnel, varies | any internet path | cheap, fast, encrypted DX failover |

## What each part proved

**Wrong assumption: Direct Connect is private, not encrypted.**
"All our traffic goes over Direct Connect, so encryption in transit is covered" is false. DX bypasses the internet, but the link is plaintext, and the audit finding stands until you add MACsec or IPsec.

**MACsec: fast link encryption, but only the link.**
It runs at near line rate in hardware and encrypts the whole frame including BGP and ARP, which is why it fits high-bandwidth links that a single VPN tunnel would throttle. Setup uses a CKN and CAK key pair with SCI on and no extra charge, and the encryption mode ranges over `no_encrypt`, `should_encrypt`, and `must_encrypt`. Its limit is scope: it terminates at the Direct Connect location, so it is not end-to-end.

**IPsec VPN over DX: end-to-end, at a throughput cost.**
Running Site-to-Site VPN across a public or transit VIF gives end-to-end confidentiality from the customer gateway to the AWS VPN endpoint, on both hosted and dedicated connections, capped near 1.25 Gbps per tunnel. The Private IP VPN variant terminates on a Transit Gateway over a transit VIF using private IPs, encrypting traffic to many VPCs without exposing public IPs.

**Site-to-Site VPN over the internet: the cheap failover.**
IPsec over the public internet deploys in minutes and varies with internet conditions, terminating on a VGW for one VPC or a Transit Gateway for many. Its common exam role is the encrypted failover behind a Direct Connect primary, where DX is preferred while the link is up and the VPN carries traffic only during a failure.

**Transit Gateway: the hybrid hub.**
TGW ties it together: attach the VPCs, the Site-to-Site VPN, and Direct Connect through a transit VIF and a Direct Connect gateway. Private IP VPN on the transit VIF gives private addressing across many VPCs, and Transit Gateway Connect uses GRE plus BGP for native SD-WAN at higher bandwidth than a VPN.

---

## The subtle one: link versus journey, and connection constraints

Several distinctions decide the answer here.

**MACsec secures the physical link, not the journey.** If the requirement is end-to-end encryption from the data center all the way to the workload, MACsec alone does not meet it, because it terminates at the Direct Connect location. That is the single most-tested MACsec trap.

**MACsec is dedicated-only.** It is available on dedicated connections at 10, 100, and 400 Gbps at select points of presence, and not on 1 Gbps dedicated or any hosted connection. If the scenario mentions a hosted connection or a 1 Gbps link, MACsec is off the table and IPsec VPN over DX is the encryption answer.

**Every Site-to-Site VPN provisions two tunnels** to two separate AWS endpoints, and you are expected to configure both for high availability. A one-tunnel design is the fragile answer.

**GRE is not encrypted by itself.** Transit Gateway Connect uses GRE plus BGP, so if that path needs confidentiality it has to ride an already encrypted transport.

---

## Exam relevance

- **Direct Connect is private but not encrypted.** An audit requiring encryption in transit is not satisfied by DX alone.
- **MACsec is L2 link encryption**, near line rate, dedicated-only at 10/100/400 Gbps, and protects only the link to the DX location, not end-to-end.
- **If the connection is hosted or 1 Gbps, MACsec is out** and IPsec VPN over DX is the answer.
- **IPsec VPN over DX is L3 end-to-end** from the customer gateway to the AWS VPN endpoint, on hosted or dedicated, capped near 1.25 Gbps per tunnel.
- **Site-to-Site VPN over the internet is the cheap encrypted failover** behind a Direct Connect primary.
- **Every Site-to-Site VPN has two tunnels**, so configure both for HA.
- **Transit Gateway is the hybrid hub** for VPCs, VPN, and Direct Connect. Private IP VPN gives private addressing across VPCs, and TGW Connect uses GRE plus BGP, which is unencrypted unless the transport already is.
