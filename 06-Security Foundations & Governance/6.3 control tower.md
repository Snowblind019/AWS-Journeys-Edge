## What I explored

- Studied the **two independent axes** of Control Tower controls: behavior (preventive, detective, proactive) and guidance (mandatory, strongly recommended, elective).
- Browsed the **controls catalog** and the read-only landing-zone and enabled-controls calls.
- Worked the **decision drills**: Control Tower versus LZA versus raw Organizations, which behavior fits which requirement, and the management-account trap.

The point was that Control Tower is a prescriptive multi-account governance layer, and the whole subdomain is a grid. Behavior is what the control does and what implements it, guidance is how strongly AWS recommends it, and the two are independent. The traps are that proactive controls only see CloudFormation and that preventive controls skip the management account.

---

## The model this proves

Control Tower controls sit on two independent axes, and confusing them is the point of most questions.

| Behavior | Implemented with | When it acts | Region scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preventive | Service Control Policy | blocks the action before it happens | all Regions |
| Detective | AWS Config rule | flags noncompliance after the fact | CT-supported Regions only |
| Proactive | CloudFormation hook | checks a resource before CloudFormation provisions it | where CFN hooks run |

| Guidance | Default state | Can you disable it? | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mandatory | auto-enabled and enforced | no | baseline CT requires for the landing zone |
| Strongly recommended | off until enabled | yes | best practice for a multi-account setup |
| Elective | off until enabled | yes | commonly restricted actions, enable as needed |

The two axes are independent: a control can be elective and detective, or strongly recommended and preventive, or mandatory and preventive, and guidance never tells you the behavior.

## What each drill proved

**The two-axis grid.**
Behavior is how the control acts and what implements it: preventive is an SCP that blocks the action before it happens, detective is a Config rule that flags noncompliance after the fact, and proactive is a CloudFormation hook that checks a resource before CloudFormation provisions it. Guidance is how strongly AWS recommends it: mandatory is auto-enabled and cannot be disabled, while strongly recommended and elective are off until you enable them.

**Proactive controls only see CloudFormation.**
A proactive control is a CloudFormation hook, so it evaluates and can FAIL a resource CloudFormation is about to create, but it does nothing about a resource made directly in the console, via the CLI or SDK, or through Terraform. To catch a resource no matter how it was made, you use a detective control (Config) after the fact or a preventive control (SCP) on the action. This is the most common proactive-control trap.

**Preventive controls skip the management account.**
Because they are SCPs, Control Tower preventive controls do not constrain the root user or IAM admins in the management account, which is the deliberate anti-lockout behavior from 4.3. The action is still logged to the Log Archive account but not blocked, so "we enabled a preventive control but a management-account admin still did the thing" is expected.

**Provisioning versus deployment.**
Account Factory and Account Factory for Terraform (AFT) vend new accounts governed from birth (baseline, CloudTrail, Identity Center, inherited controls), and AFT is the GitOps, heavily customized version. StackSets deploy resources into many existing accounts, and CloudFormation Guard and cfn-lint check templates, all of which are 6.4. "Standardize creating new accounts" is Account Factory or AFT, and "push the same baseline resources into 80 existing accounts" is StackSets.

**Landing zone choice.**
Control Tower is the prescriptive, managed, dashboard answer for fairly standard needs, Landing Zone Accelerator is the deeply customizable answer for highly regulated environments that exceed Control Tower's prescriptive model, and raw Organizations is for when you want just the primitives and will assemble governance yourself.

## The subtle one: proactive is CFN only, preventive skips management, and the axes are independent

Several tested edges here.

**Proactive controls only evaluate CloudFormation.** They miss the console, CLI, SDK, and Terraform, so a catch-everything requirement is detective or preventive, never proactive alone.

**Preventive controls skip the management account.** The 4.3 SCP exemption means a preventive control that works everywhere else does not constrain management-account admins, and that is expected, not a failure.

**The two axes are independent.** Guidance (mandatory, strongly recommended, elective) never implies behavior (preventive, detective, proactive), and questions imply otherwise.

**The SCP budget is shared and small.** An OU allows at most five SCPs, including the ones Control Tower creates for its preventive controls, so custom SCPs can hit the ceiling, and the escape hatch is nesting OUs up to five levels deep.

**Drift comes from hand-editing managed resources.** Editing a Control Tower-created SCP, moving a managed account, or altering Log Archive reports drift, and the fix is to repair or re-register through Control Tower, not to keep hand-editing.

## Exam relevance

- **Control Tower controls have two independent axes:** behavior (preventive SCP before the action, detective Config after the fact, proactive CFN hook before provisioning) and guidance (mandatory, strongly recommended, elective). Guidance never implies behavior.
- **Proactive controls only see CloudFormation.** To catch a resource made any other way, use detective or preventive.
- **Preventive controls skip the management account**, the same SCP exemption as 4.3.
- **An OU allows at most five SCPs including Control Tower's own**, and nesting OUs (up to five levels) is the escape hatch.
- **Account Factory and AFT vend accounts, StackSets deploy resources into existing accounts, CFN Guard and cfn-lint check templates.**
- **Control Tower is prescriptive and managed, LZA is for deep customization in regulated environments, and raw Organizations is just the primitives.**
- **Hand-editing a Control Tower managed resource causes drift**, fixed by repairing through Control Tower, and Control Tower can enroll existing accounts and OUs, integrating with Security Hub via the Service-Managed Standard.
