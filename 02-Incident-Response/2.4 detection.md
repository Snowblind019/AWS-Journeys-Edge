## What I built

- Generated a **sample GuardDuty finding** to drive the workflow without a real intrusion, and watched it flow into Security Hub and Detective.
- Built the **wrong answers first**: scoping off the single finding, hand-querying logs in Athena, and acting destructively on severity alone, and saw why each fails.
- Ran the **correct workflow**: pivot into Detective, validate the entity against its baseline, follow the finding group to the full sequence, pull IoCs and TTPs, and hand the scoped set off to the response labs.

The point was that Detective is the scoping layer between detection and response, and the core lesson is that one finding is not one incident. You correlate and validate before you act, because severity tells you priority, not truth.

## The model this proves

Detective sits between a GuardDuty finding and a response action, and its job is scoping: correlating a multi-stage intrusion into who, what, and where, and validating whether the entity actually deviated from its norm.

- **One finding is not one incident.** Modern intrusions are multi-stage, and GuardDuty Extended Threat Detection (auto-enabled, no extra cost) stitches individually-quiet events into an attack-sequence finding. Detective's finding group is where you see the whole sequence.
- **Detective versus ad-hoc querying.**
- **Severity is priority, not truth.** Validate against the entity's baseline before destructive action.

| | Detective | Athena / CloudTrail Lake |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | pre-built correlation and visualization to scope a finding | ad-hoc SQL over raw logs |
| Under incident pressure | fast, the join is already done | slow, hand-correlate across sources |
| Reach for when | "quickly understand a finding's blast radius" | bespoke deep-dive, custom questions |

| Step | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | pivot from the finding into Detective | it already correlated the data |
| 2 | validate on the entity profile against baseline | severity is priority, not truth |
| 3 | follow the finding group | the correlated multi-stage sequence |
| 4 | pull IoCs and TTPs (MITRE ATT&CK) | indicators for eradication |
| 5 | hand the scoped set to response (2.1 to 2.3) | isolate and rotate everything, not just the finding |

## What each demo proved

**Sample finding: drive the workflow without an attack.**
`create-sample-findings` emits a finding with the `[SAMPLE]` flag that flows into Security Hub and Detective exactly like a real one, which is enough to practice scoping.

**Wrong answer A: scope off the single finding.**
Isolating only the instance named in the finding and closing the ticket under-scopes the incident, because the exfiltrated credentials were used from other IPs to touch other buckets and assume a second role. One finding is not one incident, and the finding group is where the full sequence lives.

**Wrong answer B: hand-query in Athena.**
Athena and CloudTrail Lake are valid for bespoke deep-dives, but under incident pressure hand-correlating across sources is slow and error-prone, and Detective already did the join. "Quickly understand the blast radius of a GuardDuty finding" is Detective, not writing Athena queries.

**Wrong answer C: act on severity alone.**
Terminating the instance and locking the role because the finding is High turns into a self-inflicted outage when it was the contracted pen-tester on a VPN. Severity is priority, not truth, so you validate against the entity's baseline first, which is change-management discipline applied to IR.

**The correct workflow.**
Pivot from the finding into Detective (the one-click "Investigate in Detective," or `start-investigation`), validate on the entity profile by comparing API call volume and caller geo against the baseline, follow the finding group to the correlated sequence of every involved principal, instance, bucket, and IP, pull the IoCs and MITRE ATT&CK TTPs with Detective Investigations, and hand the scoped set off to the response labs so the whole footprint is isolated and rotated, not just the original finding.

## The subtle one: one finding is not one incident, Detective versus Athena, and severity is not truth

Three tested edges here, plus the service distractors.

**One finding is not one incident.** Extended Threat Detection stitches quiet events into an attack-sequence finding, and Detective's finding group shows the full multi-stage sequence. Scoping off a single finding is the classic wrong answer.

**Detective versus Athena.** Detective is pre-built correlation and visualization for scoping a finding quickly, while Athena and CloudTrail Lake are ad-hoc raw-log querying for bespoke deep-dives. Under incident pressure, "scope the blast radius fast" is Detective.

**Severity is priority, not truth.** Attack-sequence findings can be Critical, but severity ranks urgency, not whether it is real. Validate against the entity's baseline (call volume, geo, impossible travel, a new ASN) before any destructive action, or you nuke the pen-tester.

**The service distractors.** Macie answers whether the breach touched sensitive data, not how to scope the intrusion, and Security Lake normalizes logs into OCSF that Detective can query from its own console for deeper analysis without leaving the investigation.
