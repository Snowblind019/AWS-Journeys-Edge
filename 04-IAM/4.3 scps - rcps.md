# 4.3 — SCPs, RCPs & Organizations

**Domain 4: Identity and Access Management (20%)**
**Companion to:** `scs-c03-lab-4_3-scps-rcps-organizations.md` (the runnable lab). This file records what was built and *why each result happens*, one level deeper than the lab's terse `Result:` lines.

**Cost:** $0 in charges, Organizations, SCP, and RCP API calls are free and the member account sits empty. But unlike 4.1/4.2 this lab leaves residue: a member account that can't be wiped in one command. That's the nature of Organizations, not a cleanup miss.

---

## What I built

- **An AWS Organization** with SCPs enabled — `create-organization --feature-set ALL`, then `enable-policy-type SERVICE_CONTROL_POLICY` on the root. `create-organization` converts the account into a management account, which is a real (if reversible) change.
- **A member account** created through Organizations, which auto-provisions `OrganizationAccountAccessRole` trusting the management account. The cross-account assume from 4.1, now load-bearing instead of illustrative.
- **A deny SCP** (`DenyS3` — `Deny s3:* on *`), attached first to the member account, then to the org root, watching the effect change with the attachment point.

The point was to feel *where* org guardrails sit relative to everything 4.1 and 4.2 covered. SCPs are not a node in the account's evaluation tree, they wrap around the whole account, above identity.

---

## The evaluation model this proves

For a call in a member account to succeed it has to clear two independent gates:

1. **Survive the SCP filter** at every level of the chain (root → OU → account). One layer's `Deny`, or an allow-list that omits the action, blocks it.
2. **Be granted by an identity policy** (or resource policy) inside the account.

The SCP can only ever subtract. It is **necessary but never sufficient**, an SCP that "allows" `s3:*` doesn't grant anything; it just declines to filter it out, and an identity policy still has to do the granting.

| Control | Scope | Direction | Can it grant? |
|---|---|---|---|
| SCP | Your principals, org-wide | Caps | Never |
| RCP | Your resources, org-wide (incl. external principals) | Caps | Never |
| Permissions boundary | One entity | Caps | Never |
| Identity policy | One principal | Grants | Yes |

Everything in the top three rows is a ceiling. Only the identity (or resource) policy is a floor.

## What each demo proved

**Demo — admin in the member account, SCP denies S3 anyway.**
Assumed `OrganizationAccountAccessRole` (full admin) into the member account. With `DenyS3` attached, `s3 ls` returns `AccessDenied` while `ec2 describe-instances` still works. The SCP filtered `s3:*` out of the account's *maximum* before the admin identity policy was ever consulted, identity admin can't climb over a guardrail that sits above it. The deny is scoped to `s3:*`, so EC2 is untouched. This also caps the member account's **root user**: there is nothing inside the account that escapes an SCP.

**Demo — same deny on the org root, management account untouched.**
Attached `DenyS3` to the root, which logically covers every account including management. `s3 ls` from the **management account still works**, while it stays denied in the member account. SCPs never restrict the management account, no matter where they're attached, a hard structural exemption, not a misconfiguration. This is the whole reason AWS guidance is "keep nothing but break-glass in the management account and run workloads in member accounts": a guardrail you can't apply to yourself is worthless if your workloads live alongside it.

## Allow-list vs deny-list (the maintenance tradeoff)

- **Deny-list (default).** The root keeps `FullAWSAccess`; you add `Deny` statements. Everything is allowed except what you explicitly block. Low-maintenance, permissive.
- **Allow-list.** Detach `FullAWSAccess` and add explicit `Allow`s; anything unlisted is denied. Stricter but brittle, every new service has to be added or it's denied org-wide. The classic symptom question ("we adopted service X and it's mysteriously denied everywhere") is an allow-list that wasn't updated.

---

## Inheritance is an intersection, not a union

SCPs at the root, the OU, and the account must **all** allow an action. They do not merge into a union as you go down the tree — they intersect. One layer's `Deny`, or an allow-list at any level that omits the action, blocks it regardless of what the other layers permit. This is the opposite of how "inheritance" usually reads, and it's a deliberate trap: more specific does not mean more permissive.

---

## RCPs — the resource-side counterpart (the subtle one)

New in SCS-C03, and the distinction has to be cold:

- **SCP is principal-centric.** It caps what *your* IAM users and roles can do. It cannot touch a principal outside your org.
- **RCP is resource-centric.** It caps the maximum permissions *on your resources*, regardless of what a bucket / key / queue policy grants. Crucially, an RCP **can deny external principals**, the data-perimeter pattern: "no principal outside `aws:PrincipalOrgID` accesses these S3 buckets," holding even if someone makes a bucket public.
- Both only cap, never grant; both ship a default full-access policy (`FullAWSAccess` / `RCPFullAWSAccess`).
- **A deny from either an SCP or an RCP blocks the call**, they stack.
- **Original supported services to memorize:** S3, STS, KMS, SQS, Secrets Manager (launched Nov 2024). The set has expanded since, but exam questions are written around those five.

Decision rule: to constrain *your own* principals → SCP. To constrain access *to your resources*, including by outsiders → RCP.

---

## Exemptions and blind spots

- SCPs and RCPs **do not apply** to the management account or to **service-linked roles**. Don't try to restrict an SLR with an SCP.
- A **member account's root user IS** subject to SCPs - only the management account root is exempt.
- The **IAM Policy Simulator ignores SCPs entirely.** "Passed in the simulator but fails in prod" points straight at one. To predict real SCP impact, make the actual call in the member account.

---

## Exam relevance

- **"Admin still can't do X"** → an SCP or RCP is denying it. Identity admin never overrides an org guardrail.
- **Management-account exemption** is a favorite distractor: "apply an SCP to block the management account" is always wrong.
- **SCP never grants.** "Add an SCP to give the team access" is always wrong, SCPs cap, identity policies grant.
- **Allow-list breaks new services silently** - the symptom-style question where a newly adopted service is denied org-wide.
- **Which knob:** SCP caps your principals org-wide; RCP caps your resources org-wide and blocks external principals; boundary caps one entity; identity policy grants.
- **Simulator can't see SCPs** - if a question says it was validated in the simulator but fails live, suspect one.
