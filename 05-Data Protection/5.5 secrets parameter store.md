# 5.5 — Secrets Manager & Parameter Store

**Domain 5: Data Protection (18%)**
**Companion to:** `scs-c03-lab-5_5-secrets-manager-parameter-store.md` (the runnable lab). This file records what was built and *why each result happens*, one level deeper than the lab's terse `Result:` lines.

**Cost:** about $0 if you force-delete the secret. A Secrets Manager secret runs roughly $0.40/month, the SecureString parameter is free on the Standard tier, and `--force-delete-without-recovery` stops the secret charge immediately.

## What I built

- A **Secrets Manager secret** encrypted with my CMK, retrieved it, and observed the version-staging labels.
- The **Parameter Store SecureString** equivalent, confirming `--with-decryption` and `kms:Decrypt` are required to get plaintext.
- Walked the **rotation Lambda contract** and version-stage flow as mechanics, since standing up a live database is out of scope.
- Built the **cross-account read** configuration (secret resource policy, caller IAM, and the CMK `Decrypt` grant) and reasoned through why all three are required.

The point was that this subdomain is mostly a choice between two services, decided by rotation, resource policies, and the CMK rule. The cross-account read is where the 5.2 KMS lesson resurfaces as a hidden third requirement.

---

## The model this proves

Both services hold config and secrets, and the exam is almost always asking you to pick one. The split is what matters:

| Capability | Secrets Manager | Parameter Store (Standard) |
|---|---|---|
| Encryption | KMS, CMK or `aws/secretsmanager` | SecureString uses KMS, String is plaintext |
| Built-in rotation | yes, four-step Lambda plus version stages | no, you build EventBridge plus your own Lambda |
| Resource policy and cross-account | yes | no |
| Cross-region replication | yes | no |
| Random password generation | yes | no |
| Cost | paid per secret plus API | free on Standard |

The two gaps that most often decide the answer in Secrets Manager's favor are built-in rotation and resource-policy-based cross-account access. If a question needs either, Parameter Store Standard is out.

The cross-account read is the other half of the model, and it takes **three** grants. The exam likes to give you two:

1. A **resource policy on the secret** (account A) allowing B's principal.
2. An **IAM policy on B** allowing `secretsmanager:GetSecretValue` on the secret ARN.
3. The secret encrypted with a **customer-managed CMK** (not `aws/secretsmanager`), and B granted **`kms:Decrypt`** on that CMK, which is itself the 5.2 cross-account KMS grant (key policy allows B's account, B's IAM allows `Decrypt`).

## What each demo proved

**Demo: store and retrieve, and which encryption you get.**
`create-secret` with `--kms-key-id` puts the secret under your CMK. Omitting it falls back to the `aws/secretsmanager` managed key, which is fine same-account but cannot be used cross-account, so you set the CMK up front to make Part 3 possible. `get-secret-value` shows `VersionStages` of `["AWSCURRENT"]`. The Parameter Store equivalent is a SecureString with `--key-id`, where `--with-decryption` (and `kms:Decrypt`) is required to get plaintext back, and without it you get ciphertext. A `String` parameter is plaintext, only `SecureString` is encrypted, and SecureString is the same KMS envelope encryption as the rest of the domain.

**Demo: rotation mechanics and version stages.**
Automatic rotation runs a Lambda with a four-step contract: `createSecret` (generate a new value, store it as `AWSPENDING`), `setSecret` (change the service to accept the new credential), `testSecret` (verify the new credential works), and `finishSecret` (move `AWSCURRENT` to the new version, the old one becomes `AWSPREVIOUS`). The staging labels are what make rotation zero-downtime: during rotation both old and new credentials are valid, consumers reading `AWSCURRENT` keep working on the old value until `finishSecret` flips the label atomically, so nothing ever sees a half-rotated state. Managed rotation Lambdas exist for RDS, Aurora, Redshift, and DocumentDB, and anything else needs a custom Lambda. Parameter Store has none of this: to "rotate" you wire an EventBridge schedule to your own Lambda that overwrites the value, with no version-stage safety net and no `testSecret` step.

**Demo: cross-account read, all three pieces.**
With the secret's resource policy allowing B, B's IAM allowing `GetSecretValue`, and B granted `kms:Decrypt` on A's CMK, the read from B succeeds. Remove any one of the three and it fails. Use the full secret ARN cross-account, including the random six-character suffix, not the friendly name, because the name does not resolve from another account. Same ARN-not-alias lesson as 5.2.

## The subtle one: the CMK rule and "denied on the key, not the secret"

The third requirement is the one questions hide, and two things make it sharp.

First, the `aws/secretsmanager` managed key cannot be used cross-account. If the secret was created without `--kms-key-id`, it sits under that managed key, and there is no way to grant an external principal `Decrypt` on it. You have to recreate the secret under a customer-managed CMK, which is exactly why the lab sets the CMK at creation rather than later.

Second, if you have the secret's resource policy and B's `GetSecretValue` but forget the `kms:Decrypt` grant, B is authorized for the secret yet cannot decrypt its value. The error reads as `AccessDenied` on the **key**, not the secret, which is a confusing and favorite trap. The cross-account secret read is really two cross-account grants stacked, one for the secret and one for the key, and the key one is the 5.2 grant.

Operational note on deletion: a normal `delete-secret` keeps the secret recoverable for 7 to 30 days (default 30) and keeps billing you during that window. `--force-delete-without-recovery` deletes immediately and stops the charge, which is right for lab and test but never for a production secret you might need back.

## Exam relevance

- **Parameter Store Standard has no resource policy**, so no cross-account and no built-in rotation. Those two gaps are the usual reason to pick Secrets Manager.
- **Cross-account secret read needs three pieces:** the secret resource policy, the caller's `GetSecretValue` IAM, and `kms:Decrypt` on a customer-managed CMK. Watch for a question that supplies only two.
- **The `aws/secretsmanager` managed key cannot be used cross-account.** Use a customer-managed CMK and grant the external principal `Decrypt`.
- **Rotation is four steps** (create, set, test, finish) plus the version stages (`AWSCURRENT`, `AWSPENDING`, `AWSPREVIOUS`) for zero downtime. Managed rotation covers RDS, Aurora, Redshift, and DocumentDB, and everything else is a custom Lambda.
- **SecureString needs `--with-decryption` and `kms:Decrypt`** to return plaintext. `String` is unencrypted.
- **`delete-secret` keeps billing during the recovery window.** `--force-delete-without-recovery` stops it.
- **Secrets Manager also does cross-region replication and random password generation.** Parameter Store does neither.
